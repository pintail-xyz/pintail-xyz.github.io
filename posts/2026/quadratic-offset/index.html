A Quadratic Staking Offset: Smooth Zero Net Issuance at Saturation

Adding a quadratic staking offset to the blended base reward curve yields a striking simplification: the net yield reduces to (1−u)²·r₆₄(f), a pure multiplicative taper of the existing formula. Zero net issuance at saturation is achieved with a smooth pinch-off and a single new protocol constant.
Published

May 16, 2026

The previous post modified the \(1/\sqrt{f}\) base reward curve into a blended gross yield that tapers smoothly toward \(r_\text{sat} \approx 2.14\%\) at the saturation point \(f_\text{sat} = 50\%\) staked, with zero slope on arrival:

\[\tilde{r}(f) = r_\text{sat} + (1-u)^2\bigl[r_{64}(f) - r_\text{sat}\bigr], \qquad u = f/f_\text{sat}\]

This post adds a staking offset \(\delta(f)\) on top, designed to bring the net yield to zero at saturation. Because the blended curve arrives flat, an offset that also arrives flat will produce a smooth pinch-off — no kink, no residual slope at zero.

The offset

The offset must satisfy:

  1. \(\delta(0) = 0\) — no deduction at zero staking.
  2. \(\delta(f_\text{sat}) = r_\text{sat}\) — the deduction exactly cancels the gross yield at saturation, giving \(r_\text{net}(f_\text{sat}) = 0\).
  3. \(\delta'(f_\text{sat}) = 0\) — the offset is flat at saturation, so the net yield \(r_\text{net} = \tilde{r} - \delta\) also arrives with zero slope.

Writing \(\delta(f) = r_\text{sat}\,\phi(u)\), these reduce to \(\phi(0) = 0\), \(\phi(1) = 1\), \(\phi'(1) = 0\) — precisely the same conditions that fixed the blending weight in the previous post. The simplest polynomial satisfying them is the same quadratic:

\[\phi(u) = 2u - u^2 = 1 - (1-u)^2\]

Net yield: an exact simplification

Substituting both expressions and expanding:

\[r_\text{net}(f) = \tilde{r}(f) - \delta(f) = \bigl[r_\text{sat} + (1-u)^2(r_{64}(f) - r_\text{sat})\bigr] - r_\text{sat}\bigl[1 - (1-u)^2\bigr]\]

\[= r_\text{sat} + (1-u)^2 r_{64}(f) - (1-u)^2 r_\text{sat} - r_\text{sat} + (1-u)^2 r_\text{sat}\]

The \(r_\text{sat}\) terms cancel exactly, leaving:

\[\boxed{r_\text{net}(f) = (1-u)^2 \cdot r_{64}(f)}\]

The blended gross yield and the explicit offset disappear — they are useful for understanding the construction but cancel in the final result. The net yield is simply the existing \(1/\sqrt{f}\) formula multiplied by the quadratic weight \((1-u)^2\). Because both factors vanish at \(u = 1\), and the product’s derivative also vanishes there, the smooth pinch-off follows automatically.

Yield comparison

The factor \((1-u)^2\) at today’s staking level is exactly \(1/9\): the net yield is one-ninth of whatever the current formula would pay. At 10% staked (\(u = 1/5\)) the factor is \((4/5)^2 = 16/25 \approx 64\%\), so the net yield there is about \(3.07\%\). The net curve is always strictly below the current curve and arrives at zero with zero slope.

Net issuance

The net annual issuance is:

\[\frac{I_\text{net}(f)}{S} = (1-u)^2 \cdot r_{64}(f) \cdot f = (1-u)^2 \cdot r_\text{sat}\,\sqrt{f \cdot f_\text{sat}}\]

Its derivative with respect to \(u\) is proportional to \((1-u)(1 - 5u)\), which is zero at \(u = 1/5\), i.e., \(f = f_\text{sat}/5 = 10\%\). For \(f < 10\%\) the net issuance is increasing; for \(f > 10\%\) it is decreasing, reaching zero at saturation.

The non-monotone net issuance is a direct consequence of the large offset near saturation. The per-validator incentive is preserved — each validator always earns less as total staking rises — but the aggregate behaviour requires closer inspection.

Anti-discouragement range

The anti-discouragement property has two distinct levels.

Per-validator yield (\(r_\text{net}\) strictly decreasing in \(f\)) is preserved throughout \(f \in (0, f_\text{sat})\). Both \((1-u)^2\) and \(r_{64}(f) \propto 1/\sqrt{f}\) are decreasing in \(f\); their product is therefore strictly decreasing everywhere. Individual validators always earn more when total staking is lower, so the incentive to join a depleted validator set is intact.

Total net issuance (\(I_\text{net}\) increasing in \(f\), so that an attack reducing staking also reduces aggregate rewards) is a separate question. The derivative of \(I_\text{net}/S = (1-u)^2 r_\text{sat}\sqrt{f \cdot f_\text{sat}}\) with respect to \(u\) is:

\[\frac{d}{du}\frac{I_\text{net}}{S} \;\propto\; (1-u)(1-5u)\]

This is positive for \(u < 1/5\) and negative for \(u > 1/5\), crossing zero at \(u = 1/5\), i.e., \(f = f_\text{sat}/5 = 10\%\). For staking above 10%, total net issuance decreases as staking rises: a discouragement attack that forces validators off would increase the remaining validators’ aggregate rewards, weakening the collective deterrent present in the original curve.

The aggregate anti-discouragement property therefore holds only for \(f < f_\text{sat}/5 = 10\%\). At today’s staking ratio of ~33%, it does not hold. For it to hold at 33%, the saturation point would need to satisfy \(f_\text{sat}/5 > 33\%\), requiring \(f_\text{sat} > 165\%\) — which is physically impossible. The crossover at \(f_\text{sat}/5\) is intrinsic to the \((1-u)^2/\sqrt{f}\) functional form; no choice of \(f_\text{sat}\) or \(B\) can extend the aggregate property to cover current staking levels.

Calibration

The formula \(r_\text{net}(f) = (1-u)^2 r_B(f)\) inherits the current BASE_REWARD_FACTOR \(B_0 = 64\). Replacing it with \(B'\) scales all yields and issuances uniformly without changing the curve shape, saturation point, or peak location. Two illustrative values are \(B' = 256 = 2^8\) (scale factor 4) and \(B' = 512 = 2^9\) (scale factor 8):

Current (\(B = 64\)) \(B' = 256\) \(B' = 512\)
Yield at 10% staked 4.8% 12.3% 24.5%
Yield at 33% staked 2.6% 1.2% 2.3%
Issuance at 10% staked 0.48% 1.23% 2.45%
Issuance at 33% staked 0.88% 0.39% 0.78%
Issuance at 50% staked 1.07% 0% 0%

BASE_REWARD_FACTOR is the only constant that changes relative to the baseline formula.

Spec implementation

The simplification \(r_\text{net}(f) = (1-u)^2 r_{64}(f)\) involves no new constants beyond the saturation staking level STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS already introduced in the previous post. In particular, FLAT_BASE_REWARD_PER_INCREMENT drops out entirely — the \(r_\text{sat}\) terms that required it cancel algebraically.

The full spec change is one function, one constant:

def get_base_reward_per_increment(state: BeaconState) -> Gwei:
    total_active_balance = get_total_active_balance(state)
    total_increments = total_active_balance // EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT
    if total_increments >= STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS:
        return Gwei(0)

    current = Gwei(EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT * BASE_REWARD_FACTOR
                   // integer_sqrt(total_active_balance))
    deficit = STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS - total_increments
    sat_sq  = STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS * STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS
    d_sq    = deficit * deficit

    return Gwei(d_sq * current // sat_sq)

The existing formula is computed as current, then multiplied by d_sq / sat_sq \(= (1-u)^2\). All downstream reward and penalty calculations — participation rewards, inactivity penalties, slashing — receive this adjusted base reward without any further changes.

Summary

The same quadratic polynomial \(\phi(u) = 1 - (1-u)^2\) that smoothly blends the gross yield toward \(r_\text{sat}\) also, when used as the offset shape, cancels all \(r_\text{sat}\) dependence in the net yield. The result is:

\[r_\text{net}(f) = (1-u)^2 \cdot r_{64}(f), \qquad u = f/f_\text{sat}\]

This formula:

  • preserves the per-validator anti-discouragement incentive (\(r_\text{net}\) strictly decreasing in \(f\)) throughout the full staking range;
  • maintains the aggregate anti-discouragement property (\(I_\text{net}\) increasing in \(f\)) only for \(f < f_\text{sat}/5 = 10\%\) staked — a fundamental constraint of the quadratic taper shape;
  • arrives at exactly zero net yield at \(f_\text{sat} = 50\%\) staked with zero slope;
  • requires only one new protocol constant (STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS), implemented as a single multiplication of the existing base reward;
  • is calibrated by adjusting BASE_REWARD_FACTOR \(B'\); \(B' = 256 = 2^8\) gives a peak issuance of 1.23% and a net yield of 1.2% at today’s staking; \(B' = 512 = 2^9\) gives 2.45% peak and 2.3% yield at today’s staking.
