Minimal Issuance Reform: Linear Offset Tapering

The simplest issuance reform: tapering staking yield linearly to zero at a 50% staking ratio with a per-epoch offset, leaving Ethereum’s existing rewards and penalties entirely untouched.
Published

June 18, 2026

Modified

June 18, 2026

tl;dr

  • Ethereum’s existing issuance curve incentivises growth in staking ratio to levels which weaken security and make the protocol more open to capture;
  • This is a minimal proposal to ensure stake growth beyond 50% is no longer incentivised, by applying a linearly-tapered offset to validator rewards;
  • The underlying reward curve and the mechanism of rewards and penalties are left unchanged, but the BASE_REWARD_FACTOR may be increased to calibrate yield at transition;
  • Even with this increase, worst case issuance is reduced to 1%, and is likely to settle at a much lower value, derived from the market-determined reservation yield.

Introduction

As previously noted, Ethereum’s issuance curve incentivises growth in the staking ratio well beyond the point at which additional stake increases Ethereum’s security, and arguably makes it more brittle and vulnerable to capture. The level of issuance grows continuously as more Ether is staked, and even with all Ether staked, the nominal yield never goes below 1.5%. The current issuance curve therefore does not allow the market to reach an equilibrium yield, and we should expect the staking ratio to tend towards 100% over time.

To prevent Ethereum from reaching a problematic high staking ratio, this proposal introduces the simplest possible change to ensure that staking market reaches equilibrium yield at some point before 50% of all Ether has been staked. This is achieved without altering the existing reward curve and micro-incentives, by subtracting a per-epoch offset for each validator duty, with the tapered according to the staking ratio.

Yield and Issuance Curves

Considering our focus on the staking ratio, here we will express characteristic curves as functions of the staking ratio \(f\) (the fraction of total supply staked): the annual yield \(y(f)\) a validator earns on staked ETH,1 and the issuance \(i(f)\) — new ETH minted per year, as a fraction of total supply.

Under Ethereum’s current rules the yield follows

\[y(f) = \frac{B\,E}{\sqrt{f\,S}},\]

with BASE_REWARD_FACTOR \(B\), epochs per year \(E\), and total ETH supply \(S\) in Gwei. Issuance is the yield paid on the staked fraction,

\[i(f) = B\,E\,\sqrt{\frac{f}{S}}.\]

Linear tapering subtracts from the yield a term that is zero at \(f = 0\) and grows linearly to cancel the yield entirely at the saturation ratio \(f_\text{sat}\):

\[\tilde y(f) = \begin{cases} y(f) - \dfrac{f}{f_\text{sat}}\,y(f_\text{sat}) & f \le f_\text{sat} \\ 0 & f > f_\text{sat} \end{cases}\]

Applying the linear taper naively with \(B = 64\) (today’s value) the yield would be reduced significantly below its current level. To ensure a less dramatic switchover, we could compensate the tapering at the current yield level by doubling the BASE_REWARD_FACTOR to \(B = 128\). With this change, the tapered curve crosses the current yield at \(f \approx 31\%\) — close to today’s staking ratio — so the reduction at deployment is modest.

The tapered issuance follows directly,

\[\tilde i(f) = \begin{cases} f\,\tilde y(f) & f \le f_\text{sat} \\ 0 & f > f_\text{sat} \end{cases}\]

and no longer runs away: it rises, peaks at \(f^* = 2^{-7/3} \approx 19.8\%\), and falls back to zero at \(f_\text{sat}\).

The Offset Fraction

The offset deducts a fraction \(q(f)\) of each validator’s perfect-participation reward, leaving net yield \((1-q(f))\,y(f)\). To reproduce the subtractive linear taper \(\tilde y(f)\) above, this fraction must be

\[q(f) = \left(\frac{f}{f_\text{sat}}\right)^{3/2}.\]

The power of \(3/2\) rather than \(1\) is a consequence of the square-root reward curve: the deduction itself is linear in \(f\), but the reward it is taken from scales as \(f^{-1/2}\), so expressed as a fraction of that reward the offset picks up an extra half power.

Per-duty implementation

A validator’s assigned duties are attestation (every epoch), and occasional block proposals and sync-committee participation. The offset is correspondingly applied at the epoch boundary in three parts, each deducting \(q\) times the reward the validator would earn for performing that duty perfectly:

  • Attestation offset — a deduction of \(q\) times the ideal attestation reward from every active validator;
  • Proposer offset — a deudction of \(q\) times the ideal reward for proposing a block from each of the 32 proposers in the epoch;
  • Sync offset — a deduction of \(q\) times its sync-committee reward for the epoch from each of the 512 sync-committee members.

Targeting offsets at individual duties in this way, rather than making a uniform per-validator deduction, reduces the variance between validators due to the rare duties of proposal and sync committee participation.

Consensus Layer Spec

The required changes to the consensus layer spec are provided below. The machinery of rewards and penalties is left completely untouched, as is the get_base_reward_per_increment function. The only required change is the calculation and deduction of offsets during epoch processing.

As described above, we may chose to increase the BASE_REWARD_FACTOR to calibrate the yield at transtion. The change adds one constant, an offset-fraction function, and one new step in epoch processing. The new step uses one new helper: get_beacon_proposer_index_at_slot (the existing get_beacon_proposer_index generalised to an arbitrary slot in the current epoch).

Updated Constant and New Constant

Constant Old value New value Notes
BASE_REWARD_FACTOR uint64(64) uint64(128) Sets the micro-incentive level; net yield at deployment ≈ unchanged
SATURATION_BALANCE — Gwei(60_250_000 * 10**9) Total active balance at \(f_\text{sat} = 50\%\); set at the hard fork to approximately half the current ETH supply

SATURATION_BALANCE encodes \(f_\text{sat} \cdot S\) in Gwei.

New Function: the offset fraction

The offset fraction \(q = (S/S_\text{sat})^{3/2}\) is computed from total active balance \(S\), using integer_squareroot to form \(S^{3/2} = S\sqrt{S}\):

def get_offset_numerator_denominator(state: BeaconState) -> Tuple[uint64, uint64]:
    total_active_balance = get_total_active_balance(state)
    if total_active_balance >= SATURATION_BALANCE:
        return (1, 1)  # q = 1: full offset, zero net issuance
    num = total_active_balance * integer_squareroot(total_active_balance)
    den = SATURATION_BALANCE * integer_squareroot(SATURATION_BALANCE)
    return (num, den)

New Step: applying the offset

The offset splits cleanly by duty type, and each duty’s offset is linear in its reward component. This lets us apply it as three small steps rather than a per-validator computation that has to know each validator’s full duty assignment:

def process_issuance_offsets(state: BeaconState) -> None:
    num, den = get_offset_numerator_denominator(state)
    increment = EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT
    base = get_base_reward_per_increment(state)
    epoch = get_current_epoch(state)

    # 1. Attester offset — uniform across every active validator.
    attest_weight = TIMELY_SOURCE_WEIGHT + TIMELY_TARGET_WEIGHT + TIMELY_HEAD_WEIGHT
    for index in get_active_validator_indices(state, epoch):
        n = state.validators[index].effective_balance // increment
        attest_reward = Gwei(n * base * attest_weight // WEIGHT_DENOMINATOR)
        decrease_balance(state, index, Gwei(attest_reward * num // den))

    # 2. Proposer offset — the 32 proposers of the epoch (charged on assignment).
    n_total = get_total_active_balance(state) // increment
    expected_proposal = Gwei(
        n_total * base * PROPOSER_WEIGHT // WEIGHT_DENOMINATOR // SLOTS_PER_EPOCH
    )
    for slot in range(compute_start_slot_at_epoch(epoch), compute_start_slot_at_epoch(epoch) + SLOTS_PER_EPOCH):
        proposer = get_beacon_proposer_index_at_slot(state, Slot(slot))
        decrease_balance(state, proposer, Gwei(expected_proposal * num // den))

    # 3. Sync offset — the 512 current sync-committee members (charged on assignment).
    for pubkey in state.current_sync_committee.pubkeys:
        index = get_validator_index_by_pubkey(state, pubkey)
        n = state.validators[index].effective_balance // increment
        sync_reward = Gwei(n * base * SYNC_REWARD_WEIGHT // WEIGHT_DENOMINATOR)
        decrease_balance(state, index, Gwei(sync_reward * num // den))

process_issuance_offsets is added to process_epoch immediately after process_rewards_and_penalties and before process_effective_balance_updates. Running it there means effective balances still hold the epoch’s values, so step 2 reproduces exactly the proposers used during the epoch.

The dominant new cost is one O(1)-per-validator deduction (the attestation offset) — foldable into the existing rewards/penalties pass — plus two fixed passes over the 32 proposers and 512 sync-committee members, with no full-validator scan carrying per-validator duty lookups.

Footnotes

  1. Throughout, \(y(f)\) refers to consensus-layer issuance yield only; it does not include execution-layer yield (priority fees and MEV). Execution-layer yield is in any case unpredictable, and there is an expectation that it may eventually be burned rather than passed to the validator.↩︎
