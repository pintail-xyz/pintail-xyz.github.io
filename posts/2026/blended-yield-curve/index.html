A Modified Base Reward Curve: Quadratic Blending to a Fixed Yield at Saturation

A simple quadratic blend of the existing 1/√f yield curve with a fixed target yield at 50% staked. The resulting curve preserves the strong anti-discouragement property of the existing formula at low staking ratios while arriving smoothly and tangentially at the target yield at saturation.
Published

May 16, 2026

The existing Ethereum consensus-layer yield formula — proportional to \(1/\sqrt{f}\) where \(f\) is the staking ratio — has a well-known and intentional property at low staking: if participation drops, the yield rises sharply, providing a strong automatic incentive to return. At high staking ratios the formula continues to decline without bound, offering no natural floor and no smooth approach to any target.

This post constructs a minimal modification of the base reward curve that preserves the \(1/\sqrt{f}\) behaviour at low staking while arriving tangentially at a fixed yield \(r^*\) at the saturation point \(f_\text{sat} = 50\%\). The offset mechanism — which would bring net issuance to zero — is treated separately; this post concerns only the shape of the gross yield curve.

Setup

The current gross yield (consensus-layer nominal yield, ignoring MEV and execution rewards) is

\[r_{64}(f) = \frac{B_0 \cdot E}{\sqrt{f \cdot S \cdot 10^9}}\]

where \(B_0 = 64\) is BASE_REWARD_FACTOR, \(E\) is the number of epochs per year, \(S\) is total ETH supply, and \(f \in (0, 1]\) is the fraction of ETH staked. At the current staking ratio of roughly \(f_0 = \tfrac{1}{3}\) this gives approximately \(2.6\%\); at saturation \(f_\text{sat} = 0.5\) it gives \(r_\text{sat} = r_{64}(f_\text{sat}) \approx 2.14\%\).

We want a modified curve \(\tilde{r}(f)\) satisfying three conditions:

  1. Matches the existing formula at low staking: \(\tilde{r}(f) \approx r_{64}(f)\) for \(f \ll f_\text{sat}\).
  2. Reaches a fixed target yield at saturation: \(\tilde{r}(f_\text{sat}) = r^*\) for a chosen \(r^* > 0\).
  3. Arrives tangentially — zero slope at saturation: \(\tilde{r}'(f_\text{sat}) = 0\).

Construction

The simplest way to move smoothly from one function to a fixed value is to blend them using an interpolating weight \(\psi(u) \in [0, 1]\), where \(u = f / f_\text{sat}\):

\[\tilde{r}(f) = \bigl(1 - \psi(u)\bigr)\,r_{64}(f) + \psi(u)\,r^*\]

Condition 1 requires \(\psi(0) = 0\); condition 2 requires \(\psi(1) = 1\). For condition 3, differentiating at \(f = f_\text{sat}\):

\[\tilde{r}'(f_\text{sat}) = r_{64}'(f_\text{sat})\underbrace{(1 - \psi(1))}_{= 0} + \bigl(r^* - r_{64}(f_\text{sat})\bigr)\frac{\psi'(1)}{f_\text{sat}}\]

The first term vanishes because \(\psi(1) = 1\). The second term vanishes if \(\psi'(1) = 0\). So the flatness condition reduces to requiring the weight function to have zero slope at \(u = 1\).

The simplest polynomial satisfying \(\psi(0) = 0\), \(\psi(1) = 1\), \(\psi'(1) = 0\) is the quadratic:

\[\psi(u) = 2u - u^2 = 1 - (1-u)^2\]

This is the unique degree-2 polynomial meeting these three constraints. Substituting:

\[\boxed{\tilde{r}(f) = r^* + (1-u)^2\bigl[r_{64}(f) - r^*\bigr], \qquad u = f\,/\,f_\text{sat}}\]

The formula has a direct interpretation: the yield is the target \(r^*\), plus the current formula’s excess above \(r^*\) scaled down by the factor \((1-u)^2\). As staking approaches saturation, \((1-u)^2 \to 0\) and the excess is extinguished — quadratically, which is why the slope also vanishes.

The two curves agree at \(u = 0\) exactly and remain close at low staking — \(\psi(u) = 2u - u^2 \approx 2u\) for small \(u\), so the deviation from \(r_{64}\) is proportional to \(u(r_{64}(f) - r^*)\), which is small when \(u\) is small. By 33% staked (\(u = 2/3\)), the weight \((1-u)^2 = 1/9 \approx 11\%\), so the curve is already 89% of the way toward \(r^*\).

Anti-discouragement property

The anti-discouragement property of the existing curve is often stated informally as: if staking falls, yield rises sharply, attracting new validators. More precisely, it is captured by the condition that total annual issuance \(I(f) = \tilde{r}(f) \cdot f \cdot S\) is strictly increasing in \(f\) — so that a fall in staking unambiguously reduces total rewards paid out, and a rise in staking unambiguously increases them.

For the current curve, \(I_{64}(f) = r_\text{sat}\sqrt{f \cdot f_\text{sat}} \cdot S\), which is strictly increasing in \(f\) everywhere.

For the blended curve, the issuance derivative is:

\[\frac{1}{S}\frac{\mathrm{d}I}{\mathrm{d}f} = \tilde{r}(f) + f\,\tilde{r}'(f)\]

At \(f = 0^+\): this diverges to \(+\infty\) (dominated by the \(r_{64}\) term), same as the current curve.

At \(f = f_\text{sat}\): since \(\tilde{r}'(f_\text{sat}) = 0\), the derivative reduces to \(\tilde{r}(f_\text{sat}) = r^* > 0\).

Between these endpoints the derivative remains positive. Evaluating numerically at representative staking ratios confirms this:

Staking ratio \(\tilde{r}\) \(\mathrm{d}I/\mathrm{d}f \cdot 1/S\)
5% 5.90% \(\approx 2.3\%\)
16.7% 2.84% \(\approx 1.3\%\)
25% 2.36% \(\approx 1.5\%\)
33% 2.20% \(\approx 1.8\%\)
50% 2.14% \(= r^* = r_\text{sat} \approx 2.14\%\)

The derivative at saturation is exactly twice that of the current formula. For the current \(1/\sqrt{f}\) curve, \(\mathrm{d}I/\mathrm{d}f|_{f_\text{sat}} = r_\text{sat}/2 \approx 1.07\%\); for the blended curve, since \(r^* = r_\text{sat}\) and \(\tilde{r}'(f_\text{sat}) = 0\), the derivative reduces to \(\tilde{r}(f_\text{sat}) = r_\text{sat} \approx 2.14\%\). Because the blended yield is flat at saturation (not declining), additional staking translates directly into issuance growth without any yield-decay offset.

The natural choice of \(r^*\)

Setting \(r^* = r_\text{sat}\) — the value the current formula already produces at saturation — eliminates \(r^*\) as a free parameter entirely. With this choice the blended curve agrees with the current curve at both endpoints: at \(f = 0\) exactly (by construction), and at \(f = f_\text{sat}\) by design. In the interior it lies strictly below, arriving tangentially rather than with a negative slope. The only new quantity introduced is the saturation staking level \(f_\text{sat}\).

Departing from \(r^* = r_\text{sat}\) is possible but requires an independent second parameter:

  • \(r^* < r_\text{sat}\): the blended curve arrives at a lower yield than the current formula would give at saturation, pulling mid-range yields down more aggressively.
  • \(r^* > r_\text{sat}\): the blended curve lifts yield near saturation above the current formula’s endpoint — the flatness property still holds.

The choice \(r^* = r_\text{sat}\) therefore minimises the number of new parameters while changing only the shape of the curve (eliminating the residual slope at saturation), not its endpoint value.

Spec changes

The modification touches exactly one function in the consensus spec — get_base_reward_per_increment — and introduces one new configuration constant. Every other function (get_base_reward, the participation reward calculations, inactivity leak, slashing) remains unchanged; they simply receive a different value from this one function.

New constant

# Total 1-ETH increments staked at saturation (50% of ~120.5 M ETH supply)
STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS: uint64 = 60_250_000

This is the only new free parameter. FLAT_BASE_REWARD_PER_INCREMENT — the flat per-epoch reward that pins the yield at saturation — is derived from it and the existing BASE_REWARD_FACTOR, by evaluating the current formula at the saturation balance:

FLAT_BASE_REWARD_PER_INCREMENT: Gwei = Gwei(
    EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT * BASE_REWARD_FACTOR
    // integer_sqrt(STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS * EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT)
)
# ≈ 260 Gwei, corresponding to r* = r_sat ≈ 2.14% annual yield

This is precisely why the choice \(r^* = r_\text{sat}\) eliminates a free parameter: FLAT_BASE_REWARD_PER_INCREMENT is a deterministic function of BASE_REWARD_FACTOR and STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS, requiring no independent calibration.

A validator with the standard 32 ETH effective balance earns \(32 \times 260 = 8{,}320\) Gwei per epoch from consensus duties at (or above) saturation.

Modified function

Current:

def get_base_reward_per_increment(state: BeaconState) -> Gwei:
    return Gwei(EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT * BASE_REWARD_FACTOR
                // integer_sqrt(get_total_active_balance(state)))

Proposed:

def get_base_reward_per_increment(state: BeaconState) -> Gwei:
    total_active_balance = get_total_active_balance(state)
    current = Gwei(EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT * BASE_REWARD_FACTOR
                   // integer_sqrt(total_active_balance))

    total_increments = total_active_balance // EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT
    if total_increments >= STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS:
        return FLAT_BASE_REWARD_PER_INCREMENT

    deficit = STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS - total_increments   # in [0, SAT]
    sat_sq  = STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS * STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS
    d_sq    = deficit * deficit

    # Quadratic blend: w · current + (1 − w) · flat,  where w = (deficit / sat)²
    return Gwei((d_sq * current + (sat_sq - d_sq) * FLAT_BASE_REWARD_PER_INCREMENT) // sat_sq)

The three arithmetic lines implement \(\tilde{b} = (1-u)^2 \cdot b_\text{current} + [1-(1-u)^2] \cdot b_\text{flat}\) exactly using only integer operations. STAKING_SATURATION_INCREMENTS is approximately \(6 \times 10^7\), so sat_sq \(\approx 3.6 \times 10^{15}\) and d_sq \(\leq\) sat_sq, both fitting comfortably in a uint64. The product d_sq * current involves current \(\lesssim\) a few thousand Gwei, giving intermediates up to roughly \(10^{19}\); these require 128-bit arithmetic in non-Python implementations (Python integers are unbounded), but sit well within uint128.

Downstream effects

Because every reward and penalty in the spec is expressed as a multiple or fraction of get_base_reward — which is itself a multiple of get_base_reward_per_increment — the blend propagates automatically without any further changes:

Mechanism Effect
Participation rewards Reduced at high staking ratios (blended yield \(<\) current \(1/\sqrt{f}\) yield near saturation)
Inactivity penalties Slightly lighter near saturation, for the same reason
Slashing penalties Modestly reduced near saturation
Sync committee & proposer rewards Unchanged in structure; magnitudes scale with base reward

This is a more fundamental change than the additive offset proposals: the base reward formula itself depends on total active balance in a non-\(1/\sqrt{T}\) way. The character of each mechanism is preserved; only its magnitude shifts, smoothly, with the staking ratio.

Summary

The quadratic blend

\[\tilde{r}(f) = r^* + (1-u)^2\bigl[r_{64}(f) - r^*\bigr], \quad u = f / f_\text{sat}\]

is the simplest polynomial modification of the \(1/\sqrt{f}\) base reward curve that:

  • preserves the \(1/\sqrt{f}\) shape exactly at zero staking, and approximately at low staking ratios;
  • arrives at a chosen target yield \(r^*\) at saturation with zero slope — no cliff, no kink;
  • maintains the anti-discouragement property (total issuance strictly increasing in staking) throughout the full range \([0, f_\text{sat}]\).

With a staking offset applied on top of this modified gross yield, net issuance could be brought smoothly to zero at saturation — without the residual slope that afflicts polynomial offsets applied to the existing \(1/\sqrt{f}\) curve.
