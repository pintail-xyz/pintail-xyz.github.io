Issuance Tapering via a Staking Offset for Ethereum

A minimal modification to Ethereum’s issuance curve that tapers new issuance to zero at a 50% staking ratio while preserving consensus penalties throughout.
Published

May 10, 2026

[Intro paragraph — reference to staking ratio post; motivation for proposing a specific curve.]

tl;dr

  • A staking offset is subtracted from each validator’s attestation reward; it grows linearly from zero to the base reward as total staking grows from 0 to 50%, tapering net issuance to zero at the 50% threshold.
  • The net yield at the current \(\sim\!33\%\) staking ratio is approximately 91% of today’s level — a modest reduction at deployment.
  • Only two spec changes are required: BASE_REWARD_FACTOR is updated to 128, and a single new function get_base_reward_offset_per_increment is added.

Analytic Form

Let \(f\) denote the staking ratio (ETH staked / total ETH supply) and let \(r(f)\) and \(I(f)\) be the consensus-layer nominal yield and total annual issuance under the current curve:

\[r(f) = \frac{B \cdot E}{\sqrt{f \cdot S \cdot 10^9}}, \qquad I(f) = r(f) \cdot f \cdot S = B \cdot E \sqrt{\frac{f \cdot S}{10^9}}\]

where \(B = 64\) (BASE_REWARD_FACTOR), \(E \approx 82{,}181\) (epochs per year), and \(S \approx 120.5 \times 10^6\) ETH (total supply).

The proposed modification introduces a staking offset \(o(f)\) that is subtracted from validator rewards but not from penalties. The offset is set so that it equals the base reward at the saturation point \(f_\text{sat} = \tfrac{1}{2}\), and scales linearly with \(f\) below that:

\[o(f) = \frac{f}{f_\text{sat}}\,r(f_\text{sat}) = 2f \cdot r\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\]

The gross reward is scaled by a factor of 2, so both the gross reward and the offset are doubled relative to the current base reward:

\[r_\text{new}(f) = \max\!\left(0,\; 2\,r(f) - 2\,o(f)\right) = 2 \cdot \max\!\left(0,\; r(f) - o(f)\right), \qquad I_\text{new}(f) = r_\text{new}(f) \cdot f \cdot S\]

By construction, \(r_\text{new}(f_\text{sat}) = 0\): at 50% staking the offset exactly cancels the gross reward, and net issuance falls to zero.

Effective multiplier. Since \(r(f) \propto f^{-1/2}\), the ratio \(r(f_\text{sat})/r(f) = \sqrt{f/f_\text{sat}}\), so:

\[o(f) = r(f) \cdot \left(\frac{f}{f_\text{sat}}\right)^{3/2}\]

This gives a clean multiplicative form:

\[r_\text{new}(f) = 2\,r(f)\cdot\max\!\left(0,\;1 - (2f)^{3/2}\right), \qquad I_\text{new}(f) = 2\,I(f)\cdot\max\!\left(0,\;1 - (2f)^{3/2}\right)\]

At the current staking ratio \(f_0 \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\), the multiplier evaluates to \(2\bigl(1-(2/3)^{3/2}\bigr) \approx 0.91\), so the proposed curve reduces issuance by about 9% relative to today at deployment.

Properties

Yield

Dilution-Adjusted Yield

New issuance increases the total ETH supply, diluting all holders by the annual inflation rate \(I(f)/S = r(f) \cdot f\). The dilution-adjusted yield — the net return to a staker after accounting for this inflation — is:

\[r_\text{adj}(f) = r(f)\,(1 - f)\]

As discussed in the staking ratio post, the gap between the nominal and dilution-adjusted yield is distortive, particularly for solo stakers. The chart below shows both yields for the current curve and the proposed curve: solid lines give the nominal yield; dashed lines give the dilution-adjusted yield. The staking offset reduces this gap at all staking ratios and eliminates it entirely at \(f = \tfrac{1}{2}\).

Total Issuance

Anti-Discouragement Attacks

Resistance to discouragement attacks was an explicit design criterion for the current issuance curve prior to the beacon chain launch. In a discouragement attack, an adversary forces validators offline — at some cost to itself — in order to increase its share of a shrinking reward pool, with the ultimate goal of reducing the honest validator set below the threshold needed for a 51% attack. Under the current curve, total issuance \(I(f) \propto \sqrt{f}\) is strictly increasing in \(f\), so exits reduce the total reward pool: the attacker’s relative gain from shrinking the validator set never exceeds its cost.

Under the proposed curve:

\[I_\text{new}(f) \propto 2\sqrt{f}\left(1 - (2f)^{3/2}\right) = 2\left(\sqrt{f} - 2\sqrt{2}\,f^2\right)\]

Differentiating:

\[\frac{dI_\text{new}}{df} \propto \frac{1}{2\sqrt{f}} - 4\sqrt{2}\,f\]

This is positive for \(f < f^*\), where \(f^* = 2^{-7/3} \approx 19.8\%\). The anti-discouragement property is therefore preserved for all staking ratios below approximately one-fifth — comfortably below the current \(\sim\!33\%\) level, and well below the threshold needed for a successful discouragement attack. Above \(f^*\), total issuance falls as staking grows, providing a continuous natural disincentive against excessive staking.

Equilibrium Staking Ratio

Consensus Layer Spec

The staking offset translates to two minimal changes to the Ethereum consensus layer spec: BASE_REWARD_FACTOR is updated from 64 to 128, and a single new function get_base_reward_offset_per_increment is added. get_base_reward_per_increment itself is unchanged — it continues to use BASE_REWARD_FACTOR, which now equals 128.

Updated Constant and New Constant

One existing constant is updated and one new constant is added.

Constant Old value New value Notes
BASE_REWARD_FACTOR uint64(64) uint64(128) Doubles the base reward; also doubles penalties
SATURATION_BALANCE — Gwei(60_250_000 * 10**9) Total active balance at which issuance falls to zero; set at the hard fork to approximately half the current ETH supply

SATURATION_BALANCE encodes \(f_\mathrm{sat} \cdot S\) in Gwei.

New Function

One new function is added to the spec:

def get_base_reward_offset_per_increment(state: BeaconState) -> Gwei:
    return Gwei(
        EFFECTIVE_BALANCE_INCREMENT * BASE_REWARD_FACTOR * get_total_active_balance(state)
        // (SATURATION_BALANCE * integer_squareroot(SATURATION_BALANCE))
    )

This grows linearly with total_active_balance from zero (at zero staking) to get_base_reward_per_increment (at SATURATION_BALANCE). Beyond that point the offset exceeds the base reward, so clamping to zero is handled in the application layer below.

Application

Wherever a positive attestation reward is credited — in get_flag_index_deltas for each duty flag — the per-increment values are combined with the result floored at zero:

# For each participating validator with n_increments of effective balance:
base_reward = n_increments * get_base_reward_per_increment(state)
offset      = n_increments * get_base_reward_offset_per_increment(state)
reward      = max(0, base_reward - offset)

The penalty for non-participation is unchanged in form:

# For each non-participating validator:
penalty = n_increments * get_base_reward_per_increment(state)

With BASE_REWARD_FACTOR = 128, penalties are twice the pre-fork level, further strengthening the incentive to attest correctly.

Verification

At total_active_balance = SATURATION_BALANCE:

\[\texttt{offset} = \frac{\texttt{INC} \times \texttt{BRF} \times \texttt{SAT\_BAL}}{\texttt{SAT\_BAL} \times \sqrt{\texttt{SAT\_BAL}}} = \frac{\texttt{INC} \times \texttt{BRF}}{\sqrt{\texttt{SAT\_BAL}}} = \texttt{get\_base\_reward\_per\_increment}\]

The offset exactly equals the gross reward at the saturation point, so net issuance falls to zero there. At lower balances the offset is proportionally smaller; at higher balances the net reward clamps to zero while penalties remain active.
