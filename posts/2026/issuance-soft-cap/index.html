Continuous Issuance Soft Caps for Ethereum

Two families of staking offset functions that reduce Ethereum issuance smoothly to near-zero at 50% staking and exactly zero at 100%, without any hard cutoff.
Published

May 10, 2026

The linear issuance tapering proposal introduces a staking offset to taper net issuance to zero at a hard saturation point of 50% staking. But a hard cutoff is not the only option. If the goal is to strongly discourage staking ratios above 50% while avoiding the abruptness of a cliff, a continuous offset function that brings issuance to near zero by 50% and exactly zero at 100% may be preferable. This article examines two natural families with this property.

tl;dr

  • A power-law multiplier \(m(f) = (1-f)^n\) scales smoothly from 1 at zero staking to zero at full staking, with \(n\) controlling how aggressively it tapers in the mid-range.
  • An exponential odds-ratio multiplier \(m(f) = e^{-k f/(1-f)}\) introduces a qualitatively different shape: nearly flat at low staking, then rapidly collapsing as the staking odds ratio \(f/(1-f)\) diverges toward 100%.
  • Neither family has a hard cliff: both are continuous and differentiable everywhere on \((0, 1)\), and both reach exactly zero only asymptotically as \(f \to 1\).
  • The key design question is how much residual issuance remains at 50% staking — a proxy for how hard the soft cap bites near the threshold most commonly discussed as problematic.

Setup

As in the companion articles, \(f\) denotes the staking ratio (ETH staked / total supply \(S\)), and the current consensus-layer yield is:

\[r(f) = \frac{B \cdot E}{\sqrt{f \cdot S \cdot 10^9}}\]

where \(B = 64\) (BASE_REWARD_FACTOR), \(E \approx 82{,}181\) (epochs per year), \(S \approx 120.5 \times 10^6\) ETH.

Each proposal applies a yield multiplier \(m(f) \in [0, 1]\), giving:

\[r_\text{new}(f) = m(f)\,r(f), \qquad I_\text{new}(f) = m(f)\,I(f)\]

As discussed in the staking offset article, the multiplier is applied only to rewards; the base reward governing penalties is unchanged. The incentive to attest correctly — the sum of the foregone reward and the avoided penalty — is therefore never less than the full base reward, regardless of \(f\).

Option A: Power-Law Multiplier

Definition

\[m(f) = (1-f)^n, \qquad n > 0\]

The factor \((1-f)\) is the fraction of ETH that remains unstaked: a natural proxy for the non-staker security margin. Raising it to a power \(n\) tunes how sharply rewards are reduced as staking grows.

Properties

Total annual issuance is \(I_n(f) \propto (1-f)^n \sqrt{f}\). Setting \(dI_n/df = 0\):

\[\frac{dI_n}{df} \propto \frac{1-f}{2} - nf = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; f^* = \frac{1}{2n+1}\]

The anti-discouragement property (total issuance increasing in \(f\)) holds for all \(f < f^*\); above \(f^*\), total issuance falls as staking grows, providing a continuous natural disincentive.

\(n\) \(f^*\) \(m(\tfrac{1}{2})\) Interpretation
\(1\) \(33.3\%\) \(50\%\) Non-staker margin; \(f^*\) coincides with current staking level
\(2\) \(20\%\) \(25\%\) Quadratic taper; noticeably stronger above 33%
\(4\) \(11.1\%\) \(6.25\%\) Very aggressive; near-zero residual at 50%

The \(n = 1\) case has a notable interpretation: the modified yield \(r(f)(1-f)\) is identical to the dilution-adjusted yield under the current curve. Validators’ nominal reward exactly compensates for the inflation they impose on the supply — no more. For \(n = 1\), \(f^* = \tfrac{1}{3}\) coincides with the current staking ratio, so issuance is already at its natural peak today.

Option B: Exponential Odds-Ratio Multiplier

Definition

\[m(f) = \exp\!\left(-k\,\frac{f}{1-f}\right), \qquad k > 0\]

The quantity \(f/(1-f)\) is the staking odds ratio: how much ETH is staked relative to the amount that remains unstaked. It equals 1 at \(f = 50\%\), rises steeply above 50%, and diverges to infinity as \(f \to 1\). The exponential of minus this ratio is therefore nearly 1 at low staking ratios (where the odds ratio is small) but collapses rapidly once staking crosses the 50% mark — precisely where the economic and security arguments against excessive staking are strongest.

At \(f = 0\): \(m(0) = e^0 = 1\) (no modification). As \(f \to 1\): \(m(f) \to 0\) (exponentially). At \(f = \tfrac{1}{2}\): \(m(\tfrac{1}{2}) = e^{-k}\) — the residual at the 50% threshold.

Properties

Total annual issuance is \(I_k(f) \propto \exp(-kf/(1-f)) \cdot \sqrt{f}\). Setting \(dI_k/df = 0\) and writing \(g = f/(1-f)\) for the odds ratio:

\[\frac{dI_k}{df} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{k}{(1-f)^2} = \frac{1}{2f} \;\Longrightarrow\; 2kf = (1-f)^2\]

Expanding: \(f^2 - (2+2k)f + 1 = 0\), so \(f^* = (1+k) - \sqrt{k(k+2)}\).

\(k\) \(f^*\) \(m(\tfrac{1}{2}) = e^{-k}\) Interpretation
\(2\) \(\approx 17.2\%\) \(\approx 13.5\%\) Moderate taper
\(3\) \(\approx 12.7\%\) \(\approx 5.0\%\) Near-zero at 50%
\(4\) \(\approx 10.1\%\) \(\approx 1.8\%\) Very aggressive above 50%

The shape is qualitatively different from the power-law family: the exponential odds-ratio curve is nearly flat for small \(f\) (the odds ratio is close to zero, so the multiplier is close to 1) and then drops steeply once staking approaches 50%. This makes it well-suited to a design philosophy that tolerates current staking levels but treats anything significantly above 50% as deeply undesirable.

Shape trade-off. The power-law family exerts modest downward pressure across the full range, including at low staking ratios where excess staking is not a concern. The exponential odds-ratio family is more surgical: it leaves the yield nearly unchanged below \(\sim\!30\%\) and concentrates its effect above 50%, which maps more directly onto the policy motivation.

Anti-discouragement. Both families preserve the anti-discouragement property below \(f^*\). For comparable residuals at 50%, the exponential odds-ratio family has a lower \(f^*\), meaning the anti-discouragement zone is narrower. At the current staking ratio of \(\sim\!33\%\), both families are well within the safe zone for any parameter value shown.

Consensus safety. Because neither family modifies get_base_reward_per_increment, attestation penalties remain fully intact at all staking ratios. The incentive to perform consensus duties is unchanged.
